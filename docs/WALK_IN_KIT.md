# Lift — Walk-in kit

Everything Matthew needs in the truck to work a route of independent shops in the Upstate.
Goal: **the first 10 shops actually using Lift.** Not signups — users.

Companion docs: `PERSONA.md` (who Mike is), `BROCHURE_COPY.md` / `BROCHURE_COPY_B.md`
(the leave-behind), `FB_GROUP_OUTREACH.md` (the online half).

**Prerequisite — do not start the route until the Lift 10DLC campaign is approved.** The
demo ends with a text going to a customer. If texting isn't live, you are demoing a promise.

---

## 1. The 30 seconds

Do not pitch. Ask a question, then show the trick.

> **"Hey — you the owner?"**
>
> *(If not: "When's he usually around?" Write it down. Leave a sheet. Go.)*
>
> **"I'll be quick, I know you're working. What do you write your repair orders on?"**
>
> *(Listen. Legal pad, Notes app, a shop system, whatever. Don't react to the answer.)*
>
> **"Can I show you something? Takes twenty seconds."**
>
> *(Demo — section 2.)*
>
> **"Next car that comes in, write it up on this instead of your pad. Costs you nothing,
> I'll set it up for you sitting right here. If it's not faster, throw this away."**
>
> *(Hand him the brochure. Get a cell number or an email. Write it down in front of him.)*
>
> **"I'll swing back in a couple weeks."**

**Rules**
- Ninety seconds max unless *he* is asking questions. When he starts asking, shut up and answer.
- If he's under a car, do not make him come out. "I'll leave this on the counter — when's a good time?"
- Never say: AI, platform, solution, SaaS, onboarding, seamless, workflow, digital.
- Never badmouth Shopmonkey or Tekmetric. He may have picked one. You're not there to make him wrong.
- Do not explain features. The demo explains the features.
- Ask for one repair order. Never ask for "a trial."

## 2. The demo — choreography

This is the only thing you have that he has never seen before. It is worth more than every
word on the brochure. Rehearse it until it works on the first try in a loud bay with one bar.

**Before you get out of the truck**
- App open, logged into the **demo shop** — not the QA shop with Dale's junk data in it.
- Sitting on New RO with a customer and vehicle already picked, so you land straight on
  concern / line items.
- Brightness at 100%, volume up, Do Not Disturb on.
- Screen unlocked and awake. Fumbling with Face ID in front of him kills it.

**The move**
1. **"Say you just pulled the wheels off a Camry."**
2. Tap the mic. Say out loud, normal shop voice:
   *"Front pads and rotors, call it two and a half hours, plus a set of pads and two rotors."*
3. Turn the screen to him. Line items, hours at his rate, parts, total.
   **"That's your repair order."**
4. **"Now watch."** Tap Send estimate.
5. Show the text preview. **"That's what your customer gets. You read it before it goes —
   nothing goes out that you didn't approve."**
6. **Hand him the phone.** Stop talking.

Handing him the phone is the close. Let him hold it and scroll. Say nothing for five seconds.

**Fallbacks**
- **No signal:** a saved screenshot sequence in Photos. Swipe through it. Say "signal's junk
  in here, but this is it." Do not apologize twice.
- **Mic misfires:** type it instead. Still faster than his pad. "Works either way."
- **He's skeptical of talking to a phone:** skip voice entirely, type it, then say
  "and if you'd rather talk, it does that too."

## 3. The four objections

### "I've already got Shopmonkey / Tekmetric / AutoLeap"
He is probably not your customer. Do not fight for him — it wastes the visit and reads as desperate.

> "Good — then you don't need me. What are you paying for it, if you don't mind?"
> *(Usually $200–400/mo.)*
> "Do you use all of it?"
> *(Almost always no. Let the silence sit.)*
> "If it's working, keep it. I'm $79 flat if you ever get tired of the bill."

Leave the sheet. Move on within 60 seconds. Your customer is the guy with a legal pad.

### "My customers don't text" / "my guys are older"
This is a real objection and the reframe is the whole answer.

> "Fair enough. How do they reach you now?"
> *(Phone. Which he answers under a car.)*
> "The texting part you can shut off. The part that saves you is writing the ticket by
> talking instead of by hand. The texting's a bonus if your customers want it."

Lift is an RO tool that also texts — not a texting tool. Sell it that way to this guy.

### "I'm too old for this" / "I'm not a computer guy"
> "That's the whole point — there's no computer. It's your phone, and you talk to it."
> "Setup's three screens, about ten minutes, and I'll do it sitting right here."

Never say "it's easy to use." Show, don't claim. If he's still hesitant, do the setup on the
spot — that turns the objection into the close.

### "What's it cost?"
> "Seventy-nine a month, flat. No per-tech fees, no contract."
> "But for you — nothing for six months. I'd rather have ten shops using it and telling me
> what's broken than ten shops paying me."

If he pushes back on free ("what's the catch"):
> "No catch. You're one of the first ten. I need you to tell me what's wrong with it, and I
> need to be able to say your shop uses it. That's the trade."

Then lock it: **founding shops keep $49/mo forever when the free period ends.** Say the
number out loud so it's real. Do not discount below it later for anyone.

### Bonus answers to have ready
- **"Who are you?"** — "Matthew. I live in Greer, I built this thing. Not a salesman, not a
  call center — that's my cell on the back of that sheet."
- **"Does it do QuickBooks?"** — "Not automatically. It exports a file that imports. If
  that's a dealbreaker for you, tell me and I'll look at building it." *(Write it down if he says it.)*
- **"What happens to my data if I quit?"** — "You download all of it, any time, one click.
  Even after you cancel." *(This is one of Mike's top fears — say it even unprompted.)*
- **"Can my part-timer use it?"** — "Yeah, free. No per-user charges, ever."
- **"Can I take card payments?"** — "Yes, through Stripe — takes about five minutes to
  connect your bank. And you can just mark it paid if they hand you cash or a check."

## 4. What to carry

| Bring | Leave at home |
|---|---|
| Phone, fully charged, demo staged | Laptop or iPad — signals "salesman" |
| 20 brochures | Slide deck of any kind |
| A pen that works | Branded polo. Wear work clothes and jeans |
| Index cards or a small notebook | A pitch you rehearsed word-for-word |
| Battery pack | Anything you have to set down to use |

Dress like a guy who works on things, because you are one. If you look like a rep, you get
treated like a rep.

---

## 5. When to go

- **Best:** Tuesday–Thursday, 9:30–11:00 AM or 1:30–3:30 PM.
- **Avoid:** Monday morning (weekend backlog chaos), Friday afternoon (pushing cars out the
  door), 11:30–1:00 (lunch), rainy days (shops are slammed), the last three days of the month.
- Two days a week, ten shops a day, is a sustainable pace alongside a day job.

## 6. Who to visit

Work a town at a time — Greer, Taylors, Duncan, Simpsonville, Mauldin, Easley, Piedmont,
Travelers Rest. Google Maps "auto repair," then filter by eye.

**Go in if:** one to three bay doors · hand-painted or hand-lettered sign · the owner's truck
parked out front · no franchise branding · a couple of cars waiting in the side lot.

**Skip:** Firestone, Midas, Jiffy Lube, Meineke, any dealership · transmission-only shops ·
body shops (completely different workflow) · tire-only shops · anything with a receptionist
at a front desk. A front desk means a service advisor, which means he's the anti-persona.

Whatever you use to keep track of who you've walked — notebook, notes app, back of the
brochure — fill it in **in the parking lot before you drive off.** You will not remember
shop number seven by dinner, and the two facts that matter later are *what he uses now* and
*what he said no to.*

## 7. The 24-hour text

Send the same day or first thing next morning. No link, no pitch.

> Matthew here — the software guy who stopped in at [Shop Name] this morning. No pitch,
> just leaving you my number in case you want to try that repair-order thing. Text me any time.

That's the whole message. If he replies asking for a link, then send it — and offer to come
set it up rather than sending him to sign up alone.

---

## 8. The second visit — where the conversions actually happen

Two weeks later. Bring two coffees. Nobody does this, which is exactly why it works.

> "Not here to sell you anything. Just curious — did you ever try it?"

- **If no:** *"What got in the way?"* — that answer is worth more to you than the sale is.
  Write it down verbatim. Three shops giving the same reason is a product decision.
- **If yes:** *"What broke?"* Fix it that week and text him that you fixed it. That single
  loop — he complains, you fix it in days, you tell him — is the entire reason a small
  developer beats Shopmonkey with this guy.

---

## 9. Onboarding a shop that says yes

Do it in person. All ten. An hour each is ten hours total and it is the difference between
ten logos and ten users.

1. Drive there. Sit in his office or on his counter.
2. Set up the shop profile yourself — name, address, phone, timezone, labor rate.
3. Pick his starter jobs with him. Ask what he does most; fix the prices to *his* prices.
4. Type in his 15–20 most frequent customers. You do the typing.
5. Watch him write one real RO on a real car. Do not touch the phone. Let him struggle a
   little and note exactly where he hesitates — that's your UX backlog.
6. Send one real estimate to one real customer before you leave.
7. Before you go: "Text me when something breaks. I mean it — that's the deal."

A shop that has sent one real estimate to one real customer is activated. A shop that has
only signed up is not a customer, it's a statistic.

## 10. The math, so the slow days don't feel like failure

Roughly, per 10 shops walked:
- ~6 owners actually on site
- ~3 give you sixty seconds
- ~1 becomes HOT

So **~10 visits per real lead, ~2 leads per active shop.** Getting to ten users is on the
order of 200 doors — about ten weeks at two days a week. The shortcuts below beat cold doors
and should be worked first:

1. **Your own network.** Whoever fixes your vehicles, your neighbors' shops, the A&P world.
   Burn these first, while the product is roughest — they'll forgive bugs out of goodwill.
2. **Parts counters.** The commercial counter guy at NAPA/O'Reilly/Advance talks to every
   independent owner in the county, and the delivery drivers hit twenty shops a day. Buy the
   counter guy lunch, show him the demo, leave a stack of brochures. One well-liked parts
   driver is worth fifty cold doors, and almost nobody in this industry works that channel.
3. **The second visit.** See section 8. Highest-conversion activity on this whole list.

---

## 11. What not to do yet

- **Don't run the cold-email campaign** until three real shops have used Lift for a month
  without you in the room. You get one first impression with an Upstate list.
- **Don't post FB Variant B** (the product pitch) yet. Variant A — the pure research ask —
  is useful now and builds relationships. Save B for when you can say "eleven Upstate shops
  run on this."
- **Don't buy ads.** Nothing to optimize toward yet.
- **Don't sell into a shop bigger than three bays** even if they're interested. They will
  ask for tech assignment, time tracking, and multi-location, and you will either say no and
  lose them loudly or say yes and build the wrong product.
