# Lift — Facebook Group Outreach (Honest Founder Posts)

For independent shop-owner FB groups (state ASA chapters, "[State] Independent Auto Repair Shop Owners", etc.).
Goal: learning + early relationships, NOT trial signups. If a post drives signups, that's a byproduct.

---

## Ground rules (these matter more than the copy)

1. **Read the group rules first.** Most shop-owner groups ban self-promo outright or restrict it to a promo day. Getting banned from the 3 groups where Mike actually lives is an unrecoverable loss. When in doubt, message an admin first: *"I'm building software for small shops and want to ask owners for input, not pitch. OK to post?"* Admins say yes to that surprisingly often — and sometimes pin it.
2. **Be a member before you're a poster.** Spend 1–2 weeks commenting helpfully on other people's threads before posting anything. FB group culture punishes drive-by posters.
3. **Never hide that you're the builder.** The instant someone smells astroturf, you're done in that group. "I'm building this and I need shop owners to tell me where I'm wrong" is disarming precisely because every other software post is a stealth pitch.
4. **Don't pretend to be a mechanic.** You're a software guy. Own it — it reads as respect, not weakness.
5. **No links in the post.** Ask people to comment or DM. Post a link only when someone explicitly asks — and even then, prefer DM. (Also: FB deprioritizes posts with outbound links.)
6. **Reply to every single comment**, including the hostile ones. The thread is the marketing. A founder patiently answering a skeptic is worth more than the post itself.
7. **One post per group. Don't cross-post the same text** on the same day — owners are in multiple groups and it reads as spam the second time.

## Post variants

### Variant A — Pure research ask (no product mention) — safest, use first

> Question for the owner-operators here — the ones without a service advisor, where you're the tech AND the front counter.
>
> How many times a day does a customer text or call asking "is my car ready?" And what do you actually do when it happens mid-job — stop and answer, or let it sit and deal with the pile at 5:30?
>
> I'm asking because I'm a software developer building a tool for 1–3 bay shops (not selling anything here, just trying to build the right thing), and everyone I talk to says the customer-communication stuff hurts worse than anything the big shop-management systems actually fix. But I'm not a mechanic, so before I get too deep I'd rather hear it from people who live it.
>
> What eats your day that software has never fixed? Brutally honest answers welcome — including "we don't need more software."

**Why this one first:** it's a real question people love answering (complaining about interruptions is a group pastime), it's transparent about your motive, and it asks nothing. The comment thread is your customer-discovery interview. Rules-compliant almost everywhere because there's nothing to buy.

### Variant B — Honest feedback ask (product named, no link)

> Being upfront: I'm not a shop owner — I'm a software developer, and I've spent the last few months building an app for 1–3 bay independent shops. Before I try to charge anyone a dime for it, I need people who actually run a shop to tell me where I'm wrong.
>
> The idea: it answers your "is my car ready?" texts for you (pulls the real status off the RO and replies in seconds), drafts your estimate texts in plain English so you approve and send from your phone, and takes payment by text link before pickup. Flat price, no per-tech fees, built to run entirely from a phone because I know most of you don't sit at a desk.
>
> What I'm asking for: 10 minutes of honest criticism. Would this actually help, or am I solving the wrong problem? What would make you never touch it? A few owners willing to kick the tires get it free while I work out the rough edges — but honestly, I need the criticism more than the users right now.
>
> Comment or DM me. And if the answer is "this is dumb, here's why" — that's genuinely the most useful thing you can tell me.

**Use when:** the group allows it (or an admin OK'd it), or after Variant A got real engagement in that group and people asked "so what are you building?"

### Variant C — Comment-thread reply (not a post)

For when someone else's thread is already about the pain (someone venting about customers blowing up their phone, asking about Shopmonkey pricing, etc.):

> This is exactly the problem I've been building software around — full disclosure, I'm the developer, so grain of salt. Built a tool for 1–3 bay shops that auto-answers the "is it ready?" texts off the RO status and drafts your estimate texts for approval. Still early and I'm looking for owners to tell me what's wrong with it before I charge for it. Happy to share more if you want — no hard feelings if not, and honestly the vent thread is useful to me either way.

**Rule:** only in threads where it's directly on-topic. Never force it.

### Variant D — The "founding shops" ask (later, once A/B earned some trust)

> A few weeks back I asked in here about the "is my car ready?" text problem — the answers changed what I built, so thank you.
>
> The tool's now working end to end: auto-replies to status texts using your real RO status, AI-drafted estimates you approve before they send, pay-by-text-link before pickup. All from your phone.
>
> I'm looking for 5 shops to run it for real — free, for as long as it takes to get it right, in exchange for telling me every single thing that's wrong with it. You'd be talking directly to me (I'm the only guy behind it, so you'll get answers fast). When it's polished it'll be $79/mo flat, but founding shops keep a serious break — figuring out exactly what that looks like WITH those first shops.
>
> If you're a 1–3 bay shop where you're the owner AND the tech AND the guy answering texts, comment or DM. If you've got a service advisor handling your phones, this honestly isn't built for you — and I'd rather say that now than waste your time.

**Why the anti-persona line stays in:** telling people who it's NOT for is the single strongest credibility move in these groups.

### Variant E — the direct "check my app out" ask

Lower-friction alternative to A/B when you'd rather just ask plainly. Converts fewer commenters than the research post, but responders are warmer (self-selected as willing to try software).

**E1 — straight ask:**

> Hoping this is allowed here — mods, delete if not.
>
> I'm a software developer and I've spent the last few months building an app for small independent shops. 1–3 bays, no service advisor, the kind of shop where the owner is also the guy answering every text. It answers the "is my car ready?" texts for you off the RO status, and drafts your estimate texts so you just approve and send from your phone.
>
> Honest truth: I don't have a single real shop using it yet. Before I go any further I'd love a few owners to check it out and tell me what's wrong with it. Free — I'm after opinions, not money. It'll cost something eventually, but not for the folks who help me get it right.
>
> Comment or DM if you're willing to take a look. Rip it apart.

**E2 — shorter, self-deprecating:**

> Long shot, but — any owner-operators here willing to look at an app I built and tell me if it's useful or garbage?
>
> It's for 1–3 bay shops. Answers the "is it ready?" texts automatically and sends your estimates by text so you can stay under the hood. I'm a developer, not a mechanic, so there's a real chance I got stuff wrong — that's exactly what I want to hear about.
>
> Free to try, no catch. Comment or DM me and I'll send you the link.

**E3 — two-liner (strict groups / busy feeds):**

> Built an app that answers your customers' "is my car ready?" texts for you so you can keep wrenching. Looking for a few 1–3 bay shop owners to try it free and tell me everything that's wrong with it — DM me if you're game.

**Craft notes for this style:**
- The "mods, delete if not" opener signals you know the norms — often earns goodwill instead of a ban.
- "I don't have a single shop using it yet" is the most disarming line — admitting zero is what separates this from stealth sales.
- Always say it'll cost money eventually. "Free" now + $79 later with no warning is a bait-and-switch story that travels fast in these groups.
- "Rip it apart" outperforms "check it out" — asking for criticism gets more engagement than asking for approval.
- Link goes in DM, not the post. First DM reply is personal: their name, one question about their shop, then the link.

## Handling predictable responses

| Comment | Reply approach |
|---|---|
| "Another software guy who's never turned a wrench" | Agree. "You're right, I haven't — that's exactly why I'm here asking instead of guessing." Then ask them a question about their shop. |
| "My customers would hate AI texts" | "That was my first worry too. Nothing goes out you didn't approve, and the auto-replies only fire on 'is it ready' status checks — one tap turns them off entirely. But tell me more — is it the AI part, or any auto-reply at all?" |
| "Shopmonkey/Tekmetric already does this" | Don't trash them. "They're good tools for bigger shops. I'm building for the shop that looked at $400/mo and 90% of features they'd never touch and walked away." |
| "What's it cost?" | Straight answer, no dodge: "$79/mo flat when it launches — no per-tech or per-message fees. Right now I'm giving it to early shops free for feedback." |
| "Link?" | DM it. In-thread only if the group allows links and multiple people asked. |
| Hostile/troll | One polite reply, then disengage. Never argue in a shop-owner group. |

## What NOT to do

- No fake "just found this cool app!" posts from your personal account or anyone else's. One astroturf accusation poisons every future post.
- No posting Variant B/D cold to a group that's never seen you.
- No "limited spots!!" urgency, no emojis-and-hype formatting, no stock photos.
- No arguing with a moderator, ever. If a post gets pulled, apologize and ask what's allowed.
- Don't mention competitors by name unprompted.

## Measuring it

This channel's KPI is **conversations, not clicks**: comments answered, DMs exchanged, and owners who'll get on... actually no — owners who'll *text or email* with you (per your own preference for async). Capture every pain quote verbatim in `docs/PERSONA.md` §16 — that's the real yield.
